Does borrowing help beyond a small destination pilot?

All methods receive the same 10 destination calibration pots, per species and fold. They predict 10 held-out destination pots. Borrowing methods also receive 20 source pots. Destination information is equal across methods.

Brier score: lower is better. These summarize predictions for observed pots, not Mars success rates. No difference is claimed to establish statistical superiority.
Understand the three methods
  1. Target only: learns from the 10 destination calibration pots only. This is a required baseline.
  2. Full borrowing: combines the 10 destination pots with all 20 source pots, assuming compatible evidence.
  3. Robust mixture: combines a source-informed component and a weakly informative component. The 10 calibration pots update component weights. Conflicting outcomes can reduce source influence. This is an established statistical method, not a newly discovered biological response.

The main mixture starts with component mass 0.5. This mass is not an environmental distance and differs from discount w in the other panel. Test outcomes were not used to choose parameters.

Fixed discount w scales source evidence. Initial mixture mass sets initial weight on the source-informed component and can change after calibration. These are different parameters. No setting was selected using held-out outcomes.

How are outcomes kept out of prediction fitting?

Odd and even blocks form complementary folds. All substrates from held-out blocks are excluded from fitting. Each pot receives a prediction from a fit that excludes it: 840 predictions per method across 14 species and three substrates.

Both folds come from one greenhouse, not two independent experiments. No environmental-distance kernel is tested. The next panel uses no local calibration and addresses a different task; its errors are not directly comparable.

Research update · October 16, 2026

When can borrowing from another environment fail?

We held out all outcomes for one substrate and used the other two to predict them. This tests whether borrowing helps or harms prediction within one terrestrial study.

Main result: for the lunar simulant, full borrowing produced a Brier score of 0.3063, worse than the neutral reference of 0.2500. Lower is better. This exposes a borrowing failure; no experiment was conducted on the Moon.

Understand the equation and result

Per species, 40 source pots from other substrates predict 20 held-out target pots. With k successes among 40 source pots and weight w, the illustrative mean is p = (1 + w × k) / (2 + 40 × w), with a Beta(1,1) prior. Held-out outcomes are excluded from this calculation.

The Brier score is the mean of (p − outcome)²: outcome is 1 for success and 0 for failure. Weight zero returns p = 0.5 because of the prior, not a measured 50% Mars success probability.

Reducing w shrinks estimates toward 0.5; it does not model injury from CO₂, light or airflow. With equal sample sizes and weights, crop ordering is unchanged.

What does this test not establish?

All 14 species and three substrates share one greenhouse, blocks and management. There is no independent greenhouse, spaceflight or Mars validation, and no environmental-distance kernel was tested.

Full-borrowing nominal 95% count intervals covered only 5 of 14 lunar species cells. This descriptive measure does not treat species as independent environments. At weight zero, wide intervals cover every possible count; perfect coverage does not imply accurate prediction.

Wamelink (2019) and Gonçalves (2024) inform experimental design but inspected materials do not supply the same day-50 pot endpoint. Harvest or biomass was not converted into survival counts.

How should the hypothesis be tested?

Use the same cultivar and endpoint across independent chamber runs. Record CO₂ and pressure, canopy airflow, PPFD, spectrum, photoperiod, substrate, pH method and irrigation. Freeze predictions before unblinding outcomes. Compare environmental weighting, unweighted borrowing and target-only estimation.

The proposed Brier improvement margin is 0.01, to be registered before collection. It is a planning choice, not an established biological threshold. Withhold recommendations when critical conditions are missing.

UNVALIDATED HYPOTHESIS / ILLUSTRATIVE EVIDENCE DISCOUNTING

Explore a transfer hypothesis

The pipeline first compares conditions, then uses same-crop success/failure outcomes. Similarity discounts the amount of experimental evidence transferred. Crop labels alone cannot supply success outcomes. This is an explicit power-prior hypothesis, not an empirically calibrated Mars model.

How your idea works — from similarity to a conditional estimate
  1. Describe the Mars greenhouse: temperature, substrate pH, air humidity and management assumptions.
  2. Find analog conditions: compare with terrestrial crop records. This identifies candidates to investigate.
  3. Find an outcome baseline for the same crop: independent units that succeeded or failed under a stated protocol. A Crop label is not such an outcome.
  4. Discount the transferable evidence: more distant conditions contribute less information, under the explicit kernel assumption. Report a conditional estimate, interval, sensitivity and missing mission factors.

Example: 20 matching lentil rows in Crop establish environmental resemblance only. They cannot be entered as 20 successful trials. To estimate lentil success, supply real lentil pilot outcomes and their reference conditions. A tomato experiment is not substituted for lentil.

For the four included experimental crops, the outcome baseline is available automatically. For the other Crop labels, this final step waits for real outcome data. The transfer function is implemented but its kernel scales have not been empirically calibrated; outputs remain conditional hypotheses.

This formula reduces the effective source sample size as environmental distance increases. It does not model biological injury or a causal environmental response; at zero weight the estimate returns to the prior. CO₂, airflow, light, substrate chemistry and pH-method comparability are not checked by this three-variable formula.

Declared distance scales: 5 °C, 0.5 pH unit, 15 humidity points. They are assumptions. Included experimental endpoint: at least 3 of 5 plants alive per pot at day 50.

Enter actual independent experimental-unit counts and comparable reference conditions. These inputs are user-supplied and are not verified. Do not enter hypothetical counts as measured evidence.

Model, uncertainty and experimental design

Each pot is one binary trial: it either meets the chosen threshold or does not. With s successful pots out of n, the chosen Beta(1,1) prior gives Beta(1+s,1+n−s). The panel reports its mean and equal-tailed 95% credible interval, plus sensitivity to a Beta(0.5,0.5) prior.

The model assumes exchangeable independent pots within a species/substrate. Residual block or shared-greenhouse dependence and between-study heterogeneity are not modeled. Five seeds in one pot are not treated as five independent experiments.

Expected information from one more comparable pot is I(θ;Y), computed from the beta posterior predictive distribution. It concerns the same experimental protocol; it does not quantify the value of testing gravity, perchlorates or radiation.

The conditional projection uses p_target=q×p_simulant. Its interval treats q as fixed and omits uncertainty in transfer. Without assumptions about q, the actual Mars probability is not identified.

MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION / INDEPENDENT IMPLEMENTATION

06 / Interval probabilities, constraint selection and endpoint pruning

This module follows the same methodological family discussed in the earlier work: probabilistic queries represented as linear programs, greedy selection of additional constraints, and pruning of endpoint reoptimizations. It is independently implemented here; equivalence to the unavailable earlier code has not been verified.

1. Define the event before computing its probability

For each crop c, select one of its terrestrial dataset rows. Let T mean temperature falls in the scenario window, H mean substrate pH falls in its window, and R mean air relative humidity falls in its window. The overlap event is Q = T ∩ H ∩ R. Neither Q nor a dataset crop label is the biological event S = successful cultivation.

T = {|temperature − t₀| ≤ δT},   H = {|pH − h₀| ≤ δH},   R = {|RH − r₀| ≤ δR}

The symbol H denotes the pH event here, not humidity. RH denotes air relative humidity. The scenario windows are user choices, not validated plant tolerance ranges.

2. Eight possible worlds and empirical constraints

Each world w = (t,h,r) belongs to {0,1}³. Its unknown mass is πw. Crop c is fixed while solving, so all probabilities below are conditional on that crop.

πw ≥ 0;   ∑w πw = 1;   ∑w 1E(w) πw = p̂c(E)
p̂c(E) = (1 / nc) ∑j=1nc 1E(xcj)

The initial constraints use the three marginal frequencies. Candidate constraints are the pairwise frequencies for T ∩ H, T ∩ R and H ∩ R. Their values are revealed from the same dataset; this is a retrospective information-selection demonstration, not a prospective experiment.

3. Lower and upper probability bounds by linear programming

Let Fk be the feasible probability distributions after k selected pairwise constraints. Let qw = 1 when all three events hold and 0 otherwise.

Lk = minπ ∈ Fk qᵀπ;   Uk = maxπ ∈ Fk qᵀπ;   Wk = Uk − Lk

Adding a consistent constraint gives Fk+1 ⊆ Fk, so the lower bound cannot decrease and the upper bound cannot increase. With only marginal probabilities, an analytic check is:

max(0, p̂(T) + p̂(H) + p̂(R) − 2) ≤ P(Q | c) ≤ min(p̂(T), p̂(H), p̂(R))

Pairwise constraints can tighten those bounds. The observed triple-overlap frequency is withheld from selected constraints and used as an audit: it must lie inside every computed interval. These are identification bounds based on plug-in empirical frequencies, not confidence intervals for a population or a mission.

4. Greedy selection of the next constraint

Δk(a) = Wk − W(Fk ∩ a);   a* ∈ arg maxa Δk(a)

At each step the code evaluates the remaining pairwise constraints and chooses the one with the smallest resulting width. Ties use a stable name order after rounding the width to ten decimal places. The budget controls the number of additions (0–3). This immediate width reduction is not Shannon information, global optimality, or decision-theoretic VOI.

5. Why endpoint pruning preserves the optimum

Suppose π*L attains the old lower bound. If it satisfies the new equality aᵀπ = b, it remains feasible after adding that equality. The new feasible set is smaller, so its minimum cannot be less than the old minimum. Because π*L is still feasible, the new minimum cannot be greater than its objective value either. Therefore the minimum is unchanged. The same argument, with reversed inequalities, holds for the maximum.

π*L ∈ Fk ∩ a   ⇒   L(Fk ∩ a) = Lk

If the stored optimizer violates the new constraint, that endpoint is solved again. A different old optimizer might satisfy it, but this implementation does not search all alternative optimizers; it may miss opportunities to prune, without changing the intended optimum.

Numerical checks: witness equality residual ≤ 10⁻⁸; paired full/pruned bounds agree within 10⁻⁷ in the software tests. Floating-point tolerances qualify the exact mathematical argument. Implementation: scipy.optimize.linprog(method='highs').

6. Cultivation outcomes require a different likelihood

In the independent pot experiment, Yi = 1 if pot i meets the chosen success criterion. For s successful pots among n, the chosen Beta prior and binomial likelihood give:

θ ∼ Beta(α,β);   s | θ ∼ Binomial(n,θ);   θ | s ∼ Beta(α+s, β+n−s)
E[θ | s] = (α+s)/(α+β+n);   α = β = 1

Twenty pots, rather than the 100 seeds pooled within them, define the trials. The reported 95% interval uses posterior quantiles 0.025 and 0.975. The estimand concerns comparable simulant pots under the study protocol. The model does not incorporate unmeasured shared-greenhouse dependence or independent-study variation.

Expected information from one more comparable pot is I(θ;Y) = H(Y) − Eθ[H(Y | θ)], reported in bits. This entropy-based quantity is separate from LP interval-width reduction.

7. What would identify success on Mars?

Without a validated relationship between Q, experimental pot outcomes and actual mission conditions, the Crop constraints impose no restriction on P(SMars). Both 0 and 1 are compatible with the environmental data alone.

P(SMars) ∈ [0,1]   (without response or transfer assumptions)

The optional model ptarget = q × psimulant is a sensitivity assumption. q is not learned from Crop or APXS. Its conditional interval omits uncertainty in q. Nutrient availability, salinity, perchlorates, pressure, radiation and gravity cannot be silently assumed favorable.
