02 / Mission transfer sensitivity

To explore your Mars question, optionally assume a factor q: the probability of avoiding additional mission-related failure, conditional on success under the source simulant protocol. The experiment does not estimate this factor.

The initial 0.70 is an arbitrary sensitivity setting, not a measured estimate. This model assumes extra mission stressors can only reduce success. It does not account for possible benefits from improved engineering.

03 / What the evidence covers

Candidate scope: tomato, carrot, garden cress and rye, based on one Earth greenhouse study using JSC-1A Mars regolith simulant, an Earth river-soil control and a lunar simulant. Source outcomes include germination, alive plants and flower/bud formation. They do not establish edible yield, food safety or full mission feasibility.

Changing temperature, light, irrigation, pressure, nutrients, gravity or actual regolith chemistry requires additional experiments or a validated response model. No arbitrary environmental input is converted into an unsupported success percentage.

Model, uncertainty and experimental design

Each pot is one binary trial: it either meets the chosen threshold or does not. With s successful pots out of n, the chosen Beta(1,1) prior gives Beta(1+s,1+n−s). The panel reports its mean and equal-tailed 95% credible interval, plus sensitivity to a Beta(0.5,0.5) prior.

The model assumes exchangeable independent pots within a species/substrate. Residual block or shared-greenhouse dependence and between-study heterogeneity are not modeled. Five seeds in one pot are not treated as five independent experiments.

Expected information from one more comparable pot is I(θ;Y), computed from the beta posterior predictive distribution. It concerns the same experimental protocol; it does not quantify the value of testing gravity, perchlorates or radiation.

The conditional projection uses p_target=q×p_simulant. Its interval treats q as fixed and omits uncertainty in transfer. Without assumptions about q, the actual Mars probability is not identified.

04 / Crop dataset: which indices can be assessed?

The Kaggle dataset describes terrestrial N, P, K, temperature, relative air humidity, pH and rainfall. Its crop labels do not record cultivation success. The following are observed sample ranges, not agronomic thresholds or Mars success limits.

pH candidate screen / Phoenix reference

The Phoenix study reports pH 7.7 ±0.5 at one Mars landing site. The screen counts terrestrial dataset rows with pH 7.2–8.2.
